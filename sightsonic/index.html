01 · The problem, stated honestly

The opening: a Malayalam, Tamil or Hindi film on a streaming service often has no description at all, and where it exists it can sound like a news bulletin read over the film. That is the problem Sightsonic takes on.

02 · Design decisions

A companion app that works with any screen today

Building inside a streaming platform needs DRM access and platform cooperation. Sightsonic avoids that dependency, and each decision below follows the same logic: work with what exists today and keep humans in the loop.

03 · How a title gets described

Eight steps, two of them human

  1. AUTOIngest

    Rights-cleared film plus its subtitles or transcript.

  2. AUTOFind the gaps

    Detect silence between dialogue and music; list each gap with its length.

  3. AUTOSet word budgets

    Gap seconds times a reading pace. Assumed 2.5 words per second at normal speed - to be tuned per language and voice.

  4. AIDraft

    Video model proposes a description per gap, given a character glossary and the style guide.

  5. HUMANDescribe

    Describer fixes names, trims to budget, removes interpretation.

  6. HUMANReview

    Blind or low-vision reviewer listens and rates clarity and pace. Their name and rating appear on the track.

  7. AUTOVoice

    Synthetic voice of the viewer's choice, or a human narrator for premium titles.

  8. AUTOPublish a cue track

    Time-coded file the app plays in sync. No film is hosted.

06 · The app, blind-first

One big button, then get out of the way

SIGHTSONIC

Status: waiting
Offset: 0.0s
Voice: Warm, medium pace
Reviewed by: describer + blind reviewer
  • Everything reachable by screen reader (VoiceOver, TalkBack) and by voice commands.
  • Phone sits next to the TV or laptop and listens; headphones carry the description, so sighted family hear the film normally.
  • Narrator, voice speed and description density are settings the viewer controls.
  • Low-vision users get high contrast and large text.
  • Reviewer profile and rating are shown on each title.

07 · Making it real

How it would be tested, funded and kept honest

Proposed pilot (8-10 weeks)

3 to 5 short Malayalam films from consenting filmmakers. 6 describers, 4 blind or low-vision reviewers, 10 to 15 listeners.

Measures

Comprehension quiz per film, enjoyment rating, listening effort, and a side-by-side against existing AD or no AD. Target decided before the pilot, not after.

Money

Filmmakers and festivals pay a per-title production fee. Grants and NGO partnerships fund community titles. Always free for viewers. Guessing

Biggest risks

AI mistakes names or events: mitigated by human review. Sync fails in noisy rooms: manual offset. Rights: opt-in titles only. Describer burnout: paid tiers, small batches.
