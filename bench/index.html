Concept note

A Memoir to public benches

After an eventful day, month, or year, one might want to take some ‘me time’. The need for this short personal break surely has its significance in today's nomadic and productivity-driven lifestyle. The solitude of “me time” affords a certain subjectivity that cannot be bound by any single mental framework and demands to be individually experienced. In today’s socio-political landscape, solitude and tranquil experiences have been commercialized as exclusive to the upper class and its very essence is tainted by capitalistic agenda. This project takes a step back in today’s perception of solitude by directing one’s attention to such experiences that are universally accessible, simple and mundane, here and now.

By using the park bench as the universal figure of solitary experiences and a symbol of contemplation and respite, the installation facilitates a playful experience of both solitude and social engagement. It is simultaneously a fortress of solitude and an invitation to socialize within one’s own comfort zone. The shape shifting nature alludes to the ever changing concept of personal space and lets the user experience time alone while also sharing their solitude with others on the same bench. Blending the user into its parts, landscape, fresh air, and surrounding helps one to truly experience oneself. The anonymity provided by the bench and the power of tranquility through the dual nature of being in solitude and social at the same time is the beauty that lies in such a mundane artifact in attaining solitude or at least a micro version of it.

The changing dynamics of the concept of personal space, and the transforming capability is what makes such a common entity stand out. As they mount up to reach this particular artifact, it transforms in form. In this way it accommodates people and their shifting dynamics about the idea of personal space in a peaceful environment by letting them with thyself or along-with based on the camaraderie. In those moments of truthfulness and contemplation, the bench transforms into a setting that can accommodate the shared quality of solitude by facing in the same direction as in each other’s shoes. In a way, the bench itself is a fortress of solitude. After all, it's not just Clark Kent who needs time to contemplate in between the mundane and the extraordinary but everyone who desires it.

The bench, in detail

Six major slats,
twelve minor slats.

Six major slats, S1 to S6, are motorised and run along the 2.4 m bench, with S1 and S6 at the two ends. Twelve minor slats on castor wheels sit between them. Ideally the bench carries five proximity sensors, each waking the pair of motors on either side of it. The installation uses three.

01

Idle

Each motor slat shuttles a short way and back at its own speed. S1 and S6 move inward from the ends and return. The free slats on castors are knocked about, so the spacing ripples along the bench.

02

Sense

When a sensor is triggered, its two motor slats stop shuttling and head toward each other.

03

Seat

The pair sweeps the free slats between them into a tight bunch. That bunch is the seat.

04

Release

When the sensor clears, the motors go back to their original places and the slats spread out again.

01

The question

After a long day, month or year, people want a little time to themselves. Solitude has been packaged as something only the well-off can buy. The project asks what happens if it is made simple, mundane and open to everyone, here and now.

02

The figure

The park bench is the everyday symbol of solitary contemplation and respite. It is the universal figure of solitary experience, and it can hold both being alone and being near others.

03

The behaviour

The bench is never fixed. It shifts shape as people approach and closes into a seat for whoever arrives. People who are comfortable together can sit side by side, facing the same way. Idle, sense, seat, release.

04

The mechanism

Six motor slats run along a 2.4 m bench. Between them, free slats on castor wheels are pushed about. Five proximity sensors decide which pair of motors closes.

05

On the hill

The bench stands on a grassy mound at dusk, with glass behind the slats so the sky shows through. Filmed with people arriving, sitting and talking.

06

Recognition

The project received a Special Jury Commendation at the XIV Florence Biennale.
